# Does the per-$k_\parallel$ forecast survive contact with the simulated patch?

Generated by `ska_forecast_check.py`, 2026-10-08. Post-processing of `results/*.npz` only: no TOD, no map-making,
no solves.

## Verdict

**The forecast holds.** The amplitude comes out at 1, the measured error bar matches the predicted one to 5%, and
`tr(WA)` is confirmed as a count of independent angular modes to **14%**, independently for two strategies whose mode
counts differ by a factor of three. The areas in `nm_kpar_results.md` stand.

## What the brief asked for, and what is on disk

**The 20 Hi mocks are not stored.** `run_hi_experiment.py` builds them, uses them for $T_b$, and saves only $T_b$ and
its scatter; the cubes are discarded. The ablation stores power spectra, not cubes, so the noiseless floor arm cannot
be cross-correlated with anything either. Re-making either would be map-making, so neither was done.

Two substitutes are already on disk, and between them they do the same job.

**1. `p_clean_cross_4` IS the estimator the brief describes.** It is $P(\mathcal{C}_4[x],\,W A s_{\rm HI})$ -- the cleaned map
crossed with the map-made TRUE Hi, which for $r = 1$ with no shot noise is exactly the ideal tracer. So

$$\hat{A}_b = \frac{\hat{P}_{\times,b}}{T_b\,\hat{P}_{\rm mapmade,b}}$$

is an amplitude estimate that should be 1 in every bin, and its scatter about 1 is the forecast's error bar. Both
halves are stored for every run and every cleaning depth.

**2. Seven independent Hi realisations.** `seed11`..`seed16` vary only the Hi field. Verified rather than assumed: the
cleaned auto-power, which is 99% floor, is identical across them to **0.2-0.8%** in every bin. With the base run that
is seven independent draws of the floor-times-tracer chance correlation -- precisely the quantity the forecast's
variance is made of. They exist only at 350-400 MHz and only for the 3-strip drift and the raster; everything else
gets the 16-bin test on a single realisation.

Two differences from the brief, both of which make the test harder, not easier:

- the data cube carries the simulated noise, so what is crossed with the Hi is the TOTAL arm, 1-9% above the floor in
  power. $R_b$ is therefore **measured from the same cleaned map** rather than taken from the ablation, so each file is
  internally consistent and the prediction it feeds is if anything pessimistic;
- the estimator divides by the same realisation's measured $\hat{P}_{\rm mapmade}$, so the Hi's own cosmic variance
  cancels and the predicted per-bin variance is $R_b/N_b$, not $(2+R_b)/N_b$. $R_b \geq 400$ everywhere, so the two differ
  by under 0.5%.

## Headline

$N_b = N_{\rm eff}\,n_b$ and predicted SNR $= \sqrt{N_{\rm eff}\sum_b n_b/(2+R_b)}$. Uncertainty on an SNR from $n$ draws is
$\sqrt{1/n + 1/[2(n-1)]}$, i.e. **48%** at $n = 7$.

These predicted SNRs are **not** the 2.3 / 0.3 / 0.09 and 3.4 / 6.5 / 3.3 of `nm_kpar_results.md`, and should not be
quoted in their place. $R_b$ here is measured from the same cleaned map that supplies the cross-power, so it is the
own-prior TOTAL arm rather than the amplitude-only FLOOR arm -- the price of making each file internally consistent,
and worth paying, since a prediction assembled from two files could fail for a bookkeeping reason rather than a
physical one. The shift is 10-20%: 1.95 against 2.27 for the 350-400 MHz raster, 3.41 / 5.96 / 2.67 against
3.43 / 6.53 / 3.29 at 675-725. Nothing in `nm_kpar_results.md` moves.

**Pull** is $(\hat{A} - 1)\times$ predicted SNR, i.e. the deviation in units of the forecast's own error bar. Every row
is within **0.7**, including the two bands and all three strategies. That is the single most direct statement the
data support: the forecast predicts its own error bar correctly, six times out of six.

`--` on the 350-400 MHz 3-strip drift is not a failure: the forecast predicts **0.08$\sigma$** there, so there is no
signal to measure and a ratio of two noise numbers would mean nothing. What that row checks is that the null comes out
as a null, and it does -- $\hat{A} = -6.4 \pm 4.2$ against a per-realisation scatter of 11.2, consistent with anything.
Its implied-mode column is measured the same way as the raster's and passes alongside it.

**The single-realisation rows get no error-bar test, deliberately.** With one Hi field and one floor, the sixteen
$\hat{A}_b$ are not sixteen independent draws: the floor is spectrally low rank -- its $k_\parallel$ power has a
participation ratio of only 2-5 -- so its chance correlation with the tracer is partly coherent across bins. The
rms of $z_b$ over bins then ranges 0.6-3.2 across the four single-realisation rows, and a single-draw implied mode
count is biased high by $\sim$3.6x (the geometric mean of $1/\chi^2_1$). Neither is quoted. The amplitude itself is
unaffected by this and is quoted: at 675-725 MHz it comes back as 1.05, 1.04 and 0.99 against predicted 1$\sigma$ errors
of 0.29, 0.17 and 0.37. Those three share one Hi realisation, so they are one draw rather than three.

## The result worth keeping: tr($WA$) really is the angular mode count

Inverting the per-bin variance gives a **measurement** of the number of angular degrees of freedom, independent of any
mode-counting theory:

$$N_{\rm ang} = \frac{R_b}{n_b\,{\rm var}(\hat{A}_b)}$$

Over seven realisations and sixteen bins at 350-400 MHz:

| strategy | tr($WA$) | $N_{\rm ang}$ measured | ratio | $\rho\,\Omega_{\rm meas}$ | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3-strip drift | 38.8 | 44.3 | **1.14** | 14.6 | 3.03 |
| raster | 118.5 | 135.6 | **1.14** | 20.6 | 6.58 |

**1.14 for both**, although the two mode counts differ by a factor of three. The rms of the normalised residual
$z_b = (\hat{A}_b - 1)/\sqrt{R_b/N_b}$ is **0.95** for each, over 7 x 16 samples. That is the whole chain checking
out at once: the angular count, the radial count $n_b$, the Blackman binning and the $(2+R_b)$ variance.

**Use tr($WA$), not $\rho\,\Omega_{\rm meas}$.** Spectra are measured on the interior of the common patch -- 119 px, 100 deg$^2$ at
350-400 -- not on each strategy's own patch, so the naive count is $\rho\,\Omega_{\rm meas}$, which is low by 3.0x (drift)
and 6.6x (raster). The reason is physical and worth stating in the draft: the interior cut throws away the patch EDGE,
and edge pixels have small $\lambda_i$, so they carry almost none of $\mathrm{tr}(WA) = \sum_i \lambda_i/(\lambda_i + \sigma_{\rm p}^{-2})$.
Cutting them costs area but not modes.

This does **not** change any area in `nm_kpar_results.md`. $\Omega_{\rm req}$ uses $\rho = N_{\rm eff}/\Omega_{\rm sim}$, the patch-average
density, which is the right quantity for tiling whole patches (edges included) over a survey. And the predicted SNR in
the patch simulated is $\sqrt{\rho\,\Omega_{\rm sim} \sum_b n_b/(2+R_b)} = \sqrt{N_{\rm eff}\sum_b n_b/(2+R_b)}$ either way, so the
3.3-6.5$\sigma$ quoted there is unaffected.
