# N_m over k_par bins, and the matched-white re-render

Generated by `ska_nm_kpar.py` at 4 PCA modes, amplitude-only prior where one exists, noiseless floor arm, common resolution,
interior pixels. Pure post-processing of the npz already in `results/` -- nothing was re-solved or re-simulated.

## Inputs

The floor and the Hi come from the ablation; `T_b` comes from the experiment. They have to be different files: the
amplitude-only prior runs of 2026-10-05 were ablation-only, so no amplitude-only `T` exists anywhere. `T` is taken
from the matching experiment on the **same common patch**.

| 850-900 MHz | `hi_ablation_f875pin_f850_900_nc32_ns128.npz` | `hi_experiment_f875pin_f850_900_nc32_ns128.npz` | drift, raster | **no** -- no N_eff/area cached; prior is shape-pinned, not amplitude-only; the drift's T is unmeasurable above bin 10 |

**Cost of that mismatch, measured.** At 350-400 MHz, shape-pinned vs own-prior experiment, `T_b` agrees to 0.6%
(12-strip) and 0.4% (raster) in every bin, against a 5-9% mock scatter per bin. The drift is the reference strategy
and its `T_b` is identical to 1 part in 1e9. This is expected -- `T` is a property of the PCA clean, which sees the
map, not the prior -- but it had to be checked rather than assumed.

**500-550 and 850-900 cannot be forecast.** `Omega_req` needs `rho = N_eff/Omega_sim`, and neither band has an
`hi_kmodes` npz, a modecheck, or cached operators (`hi_cache/` holds 350 MHz only). Building them is a cluster job,
so per the standing rule this stops here and reports instead. What needs neither -- `T_b`, `R_b`, `N_par,eff` -- is
in Table B. At 850-900 the 3-strip drift's `T` is also unusable above bin 10 (scatter 1.2-12.9 on 20 mocks, and
`T > 1` in bins 11-13, which is unphysical), and that band has no amplitude-only ablation either, so its numbers are
outside the stated convention twice over.

## Auto-spectrum, known-floor bound

`(Delta P/P)^-2 = rho Omega sum_b n_b / [2 (1 + R_b)^2]`. This is a LOWER BOUND and not a requirement: in the auto-spectrum the
floor is a bias, deterministic given the strategy and the sky, so no sky area averages it away. The bound holds only if the
floor's mean power were known and subtracted -- which would mean knowing the sky the survey failed to measure.

## Notes

### Where the information is

**Bins 3-11 carry it all.** At 350-400 MHz the raster's bins 3-11 (`k_par` 0.036-0.13 Mpc^-1) contribute 87.3% of
`sum_b n_b/(2 + R_b)` between them, at roughly 10% each. Bins 1-2 contribute **0.4% combined**: that is where the
floor is worst (`R_1 = 86,080` against a median 621) and where `T_b` is lowest (0.51 and 0.81 against ~0.88), and the
two effects compound because `T` enters squared. The high-k tail falls away more gently, to 0.5% in the Nyquist bin.
The same shape holds for every strategy at both bands. **The forecast is therefore insensitive to the two bins whose**
**`T` is least well measured**, which is the reassuring version of the `T` caveat.

### The ordering does not change; the gaps do

At both bands the strategy ordering at every sigma level is exactly what the single-bin forecast gave. What moves is
the size of the gaps: at 350-400 MHz the drift/raster area ratio goes from **451x to 329x**, because the raster's
floor varies more sharply across `k_par` than the drift's (a factor 212 between its best and worst bin, against 24)
and so gains less from a per-bin treatment than the drift does.

### Degrees of freedom: the convention question from step 3

`pk_par` subtracts the mean along frequency and drops DC, so **31 real dof survive, not 32**. Of the 16 bins it
returns, 1-15 are complex (2 real dof) and 16 is Nyquist (1). A numerical Parseval check on white noise closes to
5%: `sum_b n_b P_b / L = 0.952` against a map variance of 0.971, the shortfall being the Blackman-tapered band edges.

**The ~2x the brief warned about is real.** Counting one dof per bin instead of `n_b` halves every `N_par,eff`:

| 350-400 MHz | n_b, with T^2 | 1 per bin, with T^2 | n_b, no T^2 | 1 per bin, no T^2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3-strip drift | 41.9 | 21.1 | 43.1 | 21.7 |
| raster | 27.1 | 13.6 | 28.0 | 14.1 |

`n_b` is the right one: it is a count of real degrees of freedom, which is the same thing `tr(WA)` counts in the
angular direction, and it is what makes the sum over bins an inverse-variance combination rather than a mean.

**I cannot reproduce the earlier 12.8 / 15.9.** None of the four cells above gives them, and the ordering is wrong in
all four: every convention here puts the drift ABOVE the raster (the drift's floor varies more across `k_par`, so it
gains more), while 12.8 / 15.9 has the raster above. Participation ratios of `P_HI`, `P_fl` and the weights give 9.2,
5.1 and 10.3 for the drift and 9.4, 2.1 and 11.1 for the raster -- also not it. If those numbers matter, the
convention that produced them needs to come from `paper1_frozen_numbers.md`, which is not in this repo.

### Why summing over bins is legitimate here

The obvious objection is that the floor is `(I - WA)s`, deterministic given the strategy and the sky, so its `k_par`
bins are not independent draws and inverse-variance summing them overcounts. That objection does not bite in
**cross-correlation**: the variance there is the chance correlation between a FIXED floor and the tracer, and the
tracer is an independent random field supplying fresh randomness in every (angular mode, `k_par`) pair. The sum is
over the tracer's degrees of freedom, which are genuinely 31 along the line of sight.

It bites hard in the **auto-spectrum**, where the floor beats against itself -- which is why that row is a bias and
not a variance, and why its area is labelled a lower bound both before and after this change.

### Two things that are more fragile than they look

**The taper correlates neighbouring bins.** `pk_par` applies a Blackman window (not optional -- the foregrounds are
~1e4 times the Hi, so untapered leakage would swamp the signal at high `k_par`), whose equivalent noise bandwidth is
**1.78 bins**. The dof count is unaffected -- a window is a linear transform and cannot create or destroy dof -- but
the ASSIGNMENT of floor power to bins is blurred over about two bins. That matters only where `R_b` changes steeply,
i.e. bins 1-3, which carry under 7% of the information. Everywhere else `R_b` is flat and the blur is harmless.

**The auto-spectrum bound moves much further than the cross.** At 675-725 MHz the known-floor bound drops from
356,633 to 1,895 deg^2 for the drift -- a factor 188, against an `N_par,eff` of 70 for the cross. The extra factor is
the square: the auto weight is `n_b/[2(1 + R_b)^2]`, so a bin twice as good as the median counts four times, not
twice. **All three strategies at 675-725 MHz now sit inside the reachable sky on this bound.** I would not lean on
that: it is still a bound that requires knowing the sky the survey failed to measure, and it is the quantity in this
report most sensitive to the per-bin treatment.

### The headline, and a way to falsify it

At 675-725 MHz every `Omega_req` is now **smaller than the simulated patch**: 12.9 deg^2 needed against 152 simulated
for the drift, 35.5 against 383 for the raster. Taken at face value the simulation should already show a cross-
detection against an ideal tracer in the patch it has: **3.4 sigma** (drift), **6.5 sigma** (12-strip), **3.3 sigma**

## Task 2 -- `hi_noise_terms` at the matched white-noise level

The draft quotes `sigma_eta^2 = 3.2e-7` (radiometer, 1.5625 MHz x 2 s) against the simulated 2.5e-6, so the
residuals are rescaled by `sqrt(3.2e-7/2.5e-6) = 0.35777`. Only the WHITE part may be rescaled: 1/f is a gain
fluctuation and does not obey the radiometer equation. The `noiseonly_indep` arm is `(1 + g)(1 + w_ind)` and
carries both, so it is split first (the two are drawn independently and the `g*w` cross term is ~1e-6 relative):

```
white^2  = noise^2 - (1/f)^2
matched  = 0.35777^2 * white^2 + (1/f)^2
```

Both the split and the rescaling are done per k_par bin before the median over bins, not on the medians.

| band | strategy | draft (arm rescaled whole) | separated | diff | floor | t x for noise = floor |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 350-400 MHz | 3-strip drift | 367.0 | 367.0 | -0.010% | 399.6 | 2.5 h |
| 350-400 MHz | 12-strip drift | 494.7 | 494.7 | +0.006% | 233.7 | 13.4 h |
| 350-400 MHz | raster | 733.5 | 733.5 | -0.005% | 22.3 | 3,246.4 h |
| 500-550 MHz | 3-strip drift | 283.7 | 283.7 | -0.009% | 214.3 | 5.3 h |
| 500-550 MHz | raster | 406.3 | 406.3 | +0.002% | 48.0 | 215.1 h |
| 675-725 MHz | 3-strip drift | 375.7 | 375.7 | -0.010% | 16.5 | 1,559.4 h |
| 675-725 MHz | 12-strip drift | 283.9 | 283.9 | -0.012% | 19.3 | 647.5 h |
| 675-725 MHz | raster | 457.1 | 457.1 | +0.007% | 27.1 | 850.6 h |
| 850-900 MHz | 3-strip drift | 481.5 | 481.5 | -0.007% | 39.3 | 451.4 h |
| 850-900 MHz | raster | 760.5 | 760.5 | -0.001% | 17.5 | 5,668.3 h |

All ten agree with the draft to better than **0.02%**, well inside the 1% the brief allows. 1/f is 0.03-0.5% of the
noise amplitude at every band, so splitting it out before rescaling changes nothing numerically. It is still the
right thing to do: the draft's column is correct by accident, not by construction, and stops being correct the
moment 1/f is raised. No bin had `noise^2 < (1/f)^2`, so no clipping was needed anywhere.

`t x` is `3 h * (noise/floor)^2`, the integration time at which the matched white noise would reach the floor.
At 350-400 MHz the 3-strip drift is **already floor-limited** at the 3 h simulated (2.5 h needed); the raster at the
same band needs 3,246 h. That inversion is the figure's point: the better the strategy's floor, the longer it takes
to integrate down to it.
