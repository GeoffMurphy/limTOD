# Why does the one-dish raster lose at 675-725 MHz?

Generated by `ska_raster_sampling.py`, 2026-10-08. Diagnostic only: stored pointings, cached operators and result
npz. Nothing simulated, solved or map-made.

## Answer

**It does not.** The raster's post-clean foreground residual -- the instrument half of floor/Hi, with the Hi's own
dimming divided out -- *improves* by 2.5x from 350-400 to 675-725 MHz. The three-strip drift's improves by **105x**
over the same interval. The inversion is the drift catching up and overtaking, not the raster degrading, and all
four sampling candidates in the brief are ruled out below.

| | 350-400 | 500-550 | 675-725 | 350 -> 725 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3-strip drift, foreground residual | 6.54e-02 | 1.48e-02 | 6.25e-04 | **105x better** |
| 1-dish raster, foreground residual | 6.45e-03 | 5.90e-03 | 2.60e-03 | **2.5x better** |

That table is not new -- it is in HANDOFF under "Both strategies improve monotonically with frequency" -- but it is
the quantity the question should be asked of. `floor/Hi` is a quotient of an instrument number and a cosmology
number, and the Hi dims 4.5x over the same interval on its own.

## Per band

Measured on the interior of the common patch, the pixel set the floor is measured on. `samples/beam` is
$\sum_t \exp(-\theta_t^2/2\sigma^2)$ at each pixel, which is literally samples per Gaussian beam solid angle
($\int w\,d\Omega = 2\pi\sigma^2$), averaged over interior pixels. `max gap` is the largest distance from any interior pixel
to the nearest boresight sample.

## 1. Sweep spacing: the sweeps are not gappy, by a wide margin

**"6 arcmin/s" is an AZIMUTH rate, not an on-sky rate.** `constant_elevation_scan`'s `sweep_s = 150` s is one
half-sweep across the full 15 deg throw, so the azimuth rate is $2 \times 7.5/150 = 0.1$ deg/s = 6'/s, and the on-sky
rate is that times $\cos(\mathrm{el}) = \cos 38.19^\circ$, i.e. 4.7'/s. The triangle period is 300 s.

Perpendicular separation between consecutive same-direction sweeps, measured over 68 pairs across both passes:

| | degrees | beams at 350-400 | beams at 675-725 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| median | 0.769 | 0.193 | 0.372 |
| maximum | 0.805 | 0.202 | 0.389 |

The separation is geometry, so it is the same in degrees at both bands; only the beam it is measured against changes.
**0.37 beams at 675-725**, against the 0.5-0.6 the brief's envelope expected -- tighter, not gappier. The direct
coverage measure agrees: the largest distance from any interior pixel to the nearest boresight sample is
**0.10 beams** at 675-725 and 0.08 at 350-400. There are no holes. **Candidate (i) is ruled out.**

## 2. Two-direction coverage: 100%, in both bands

A pixel counts as seen by a direction if some sample of a pass with a distinct track position angle (within 20 deg)
puts it above half the beam peak. The raster's two passes sit at PA 126 deg (rising, az 45) and 54 deg (setting,
az 315), 72 deg apart. **Every interior pixel is seen by both, in both bands.** For contrast, all three drift strips
sit at PA 89.9 deg with a scatter of 0.01 deg, so the drift is 100% single-direction by construction -- which is
Section "Cross-linking and drift scans" of the draft, measured. **Candidate (ii) is ruled out.**

## 3. Sampling density: the right sign, the wrong size

The raster is the sparsest strategy in both bands, and it does get relatively sparser:

| | 350-400 | 675-725 |
|---|---:|---:|
| raster samples/beam | 503 | 161 |
| 3-strip drift samples/beam | 735 | 286 |
| raster / drift | 0.69 | 0.56 |

The cause is geometric: the raster's footprint is set by a throw fixed in degrees, so it barely shrinks as the beam
narrows, while the drift's cross-scan extent is set by the beam. From 350-400 to 675-725 the raster's patch grows
from 2.2x to 2.5x the drift's, so the same 5400 samples are spread thinner.

**But the size is wrong by an order of magnitude.** The raster/drift sampling ratio moves 0.68 -> 0.56, a factor
1.2, while floor/Hi moves from 0.056x the drift's to 1.64x -- a factor 29. And at 350-400 the raster is *already*
the sparsest strategy and still wins by 17.9x. Sampling density plainly does not set the floor.

**Prior dominance runs the wrong way too.** The mean of $\mathrm{diag}(WA)$ over a patch is exactly tr$(WA)/N_{\rm pix}$,
so it needs no operator:

| | 350-400 | 675-725 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3-strip drift | 0.1227 | 0.0879 |
| 1-dish raster | 0.1733 | 0.1461 |

The raster is **less** prior-dominated than the drift in both bands, not more. Where the operators exist (350-400,
nside 64) the interior can be done properly, and it says the same thing more strongly: mean diag$(WA)$ over the 119
interior pixels is **0.328** for the raster against **0.204** for the drift, both about 1.7x their own
patch averages (the interior cut keeps the best-measured core -- the same effect as in `forecast_check.md`).
**Candidate (iii) is consistent in direction but cannot carry the effect, and its prior-dominance half is ruled out.**

## 4. Footprint brightness: a 5% effect against a 1.6x difference

| | 350-400 | 675-725 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3-strip drift patch sky / interior | 1.024 | 1.028 |
| 12-strip drift patch sky / interior | 1.066 | 1.059 |
| 1-dish raster patch sky / interior | 1.048 | 1.068 |

The raster does reach brighter sky, and slightly more so at the higher band (1.048 -> 1.068), which is the right
direction. It is still only a 7% excess, and the twelve-strip drift's footprint is 5.9% brighter at 675-725 while
beating the raster by 1.4x. A 5-7% brightness difference cannot produce a 1.6x floor difference. **Candidate (iv) is
ruled out as the cause**, though it is a real and correctly-signed sub-effect.

## 5. Per-pixel floor map

No per-pixel floor map is stored, but one can be rebuilt without map-making: PCA the stored `data_cube` at common
resolution and take the per-pixel rms over channels. The chain was validated first -- it reproduces the stored
`cr_p_clean_auto_4` to 1 part in $10^6$ in every bin.

| band | strategy | median floor/Hi per px | 10-90% | corr(log samples/beam, log floor/Hi) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 350-400 | 3-strip drift | 437 | 196-789 | -0.38 |
| 350-400 | 12-strip drift | 250 | 96-633 | -0.16 |
| 350-400 | 1-dish raster | 66 | 26-155 | -0.24 |
| 675-725 | 3-strip drift | 10.7 | 3.7-29.4 | -0.16 |
| 675-725 | 12-strip drift | 15.6 | 7.8-38.9 | -0.10 |
| 675-725 | 1-dish raster | 21.1 | 10.0-44.4 | **+0.62** |

These are real-space rms ratios over all $k_\parallel$, so they are not the headline numbers and should not be
quoted as such; what matters is that they reproduce the ordering and the inversion (66 < 250 < 437 at 350-400;
10.7 < 15.6 < 21.1 at 675-725), which makes them a valid relative probe.

The raster's correlation with exposure **flips sign** between bands, -0.24 to +0.62. I would not read that as a
cause. Across the raster's interior at 675-725 the samples/beam spans only 156 to 166 -- a 6% range -- while the
per-pixel floor spans 13.4 to 34.6, a factor 2.6. A 6% lever cannot move a 2.6x spread, so the correlation is with
something that varies across the patch alongside exposure, not with exposure. What that something is, these
numbers do not isolate.

## Which candidate survives

| candidate | verdict |
|---|---|
| (i) gappy sweeps | **ruled out.** 0.37-beam sweep spacing, 0.10-beam largest gap at 675-725. |
| (ii) single-pass interior pixels | **ruled out.** 100% two-direction coverage in both bands. |
| (iii) sampling density / prior dominance | **right sign, far too small.** 1.2x against a 29x swing; and the raster is the LESS prior-dominated strategy in both bands. |
| (iv) brighter footprint | **ruled out as the cause.** 7% excess, and the strategy that beats it carries 6%. |

None of the four explains it, because the premise is wrong: the raster's sampling of the sky is essentially
unchanged between the bands -- same tracks, same 100% two-direction coverage, no gaps at either beam width -- and its
instrumental residual improves 2.5x. **What changed is the drift.** Its fixed 2 deg ladder step is 0.50 beams at
350.78 MHz and 1.00 beams at the 675-725 band centre: from heavily redundant to contiguous and non-redundant. That
is the same mechanism the draft already uses for drift vs `drift12m` at this band (1.00 against 0.60 beams) and for
the drift's rise at 850-900 (1.25 beams, gapped). The drift-raster inversion near 600 MHz is the third instance of
it, not a separate effect.

This is a consistency argument from three points, not a measurement of an optimum. Spacing and mode density are not
varied independently anywhere in the series, so "near one beam" remains the hypothesis the draft already states.

## What could not be done

- **Interior diag$(WA)$ at 675-725.** Operators are cached only at 350-400, nside 64 (`hi_cache/`). Building one at
  nside 128 is operator construction, which the brief excludes, so the patch average (tr$(WA)/N_{\rm pix}$, exact) is
  used there instead. It is a ~30-60 min local job for one channel per strategy if the interior value is wanted.
- **Footprints at 675-725** are reconstructed from the boresight track by stacking a Gaussian of the band FWHM and
  keeping the brightest $N_{\rm pix}$ pixels, with $N_{\rm pix}$ from the run that measured it. Checked against the two
  cached 350-400 operators, that recovers **94%** of the true pixel set. Used only for the brightness column.
